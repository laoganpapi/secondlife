# Segmentation and ICP

## Three bases

| Basis | Predicts | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Firmographic (industry, size, geo) | Easy targeting | Weakly predicts need — same-size same-industry firms want opposite things |
| Behavioral (usage, purchases) | Retention, expansion | Exists only for current customers |
| Needs-based (the problem they hire you for) | What to build, how to pitch | Needs outside-visible proxies before sales can use it |

Practical method: segment by need, then find the firmographic and behavioral proxies that identify each needs group from the outside.

## JTBD lens

- Customers hire products to make progress in a circumstance; the job, not the customer's attributes, is the true unit of segmentation — the milkshake competes with bananas in the morning and toys in the afternoon.
- Switching is explained by four forces: push of the current problem + pull of the new solution must beat anxiety about the new + habit of the old.
- Highest-value segments: outcomes that are important but poorly satisfied — that gap is where demand concentrates and overpayment happens.
- For each candidate segment, write the job statement and the struggling moment that triggers a search.

## Usable-segment test

All four or it isn't a segment:

1. Identifiable — membership visible from outside signals
2. Reachable — a channel, list, or watering hole exists
3. Valuable — accounts × willingness to pay is worth the focus
4. Distinct in need — they'd choose or pay differently than adjacent groups

Failing "identifiable" is an insight, not a segment. Failing "distinct need" is a mailing list.

## Beachhead choice

- Pick one segment you can dominate — urgent pain, references that travel, ability to be the obvious best choice — then use won customers as the head pin: each conquered niche supplies references and product depth that knock over adjacent niches.
- Selection criteria: pain severity, access, whole-product deliverability, adjacency value.
- Dominating a small pond beats 2% of a big one; word of mouth compounds only inside a segment whose members talk to each other.

## ICP definition (B2B)

Three layers:

1. Firmographics — industry, employee range, revenue band, geography, tech stack
2. Trigger events that create urgency — new funding, new VP, compliance deadline, contract expiring, headcount inflection
3. Disqualifiers — explicit rules for who not to sell to even if they'd buy: wrong stack, no budget owner, needs you don't serve, below-viable ACV

Disqualifiers do more for win rate and churn than the positive criteria. Add the buying committee: economic buyer, champion, likely blocker.

## Negative personas

- Document the profiles that look attractive and reliably go bad: the tire-kicker, the six-month churner who floods support, the logo too big to serve.
- Source them from churn and loss data; publish to sales and marketing so spend stops flowing to them.

## Evidence standards

- Segments and ICPs come from won/lost deal analysis, churn analysis, and discovery interviews — never a workshop brainstorm. Every claimed trait cites the deals or interviews behind it.
- Best-customer analysis: rank accounts by lifetime value, expansion, and low support load; the top-decile pattern is the draft ICP, tested against the loss and churn file.
- Revisit every 6–12 months and at forcing events; early-stage, the beachhead is a hypothesis reviewed quarterly against closed-won data.

## Failure modes

Persona theater — stock photos and hobbies, no distinct need · demographic-only segments that predict nothing · "any company with data" as an ICP · aspiration-built ICPs ("we want enterprise") · ignored disqualifiers filling pipeline with deals that close and churn · segmenting once and never reconciling against the deal record
