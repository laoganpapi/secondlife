# GTM motion

## The motions

| Motion | Fits when |
|---|---|
| Founder-led sales | Always first, for anything sold — not a phase to skip |
| Product-led (PLG) | Fast time-to-value, end-user buyer, low-risk adoption, product visible in use |
| Sales-led | Multi-stakeholder purchase, security review, integration effort, high ACV |
| Channel/partner-led | Partners own the relationship or deployment capacity; slow start, compounding later, margin traded for reach |
| Community-led | Devtools/open source — a demand engine that converts via PLG or sales on top |

## The economics test

ACV must cover CAC with payback in roughly 12–18 months. A human-touch sales cycle costs thousands per deal; it cannot sit on a $1K contract.

| ACV band | Supportable motion |
|---|---|
| < ~$5K | Self-serve only — no rep economics work |
| $5K–$25K | PLG plus light inside sales |
| $25K–$100K | Inside sales-led, possibly PLG-sourced |
| > ~$100K | Field sales, multi-threaded; PLG at most as a wedge |

Beyond ACV: time-to-value (minutes → PLG viable; weeks of implementation → sales/partner) · product complexity (self-explanatory → PLG) · buyer type (user can adopt and pay → PLG; procurement involved → sales) · adoption risk (data/compliance-sensitive → sales).

## Founder-led discipline

- Founders sell the first 10–20 deals to learn the buyer, objections, pricing tolerance, and a repeatable pitch. You cannot hire your way out of not knowing how the product sells.
- Write down as you go: the pitch, objections and answers, qualification criteria, close process.
- Hire reps only when the founder-run playbook closes repeatably — same pitch, similar cycle length, predictable rate. Hire two, so rep problems separate from playbook problems. Founders stay in deals during ramp.

## PLG-plus-sales staging

The dominant modern pattern — hybrids retain better than pure PLG:

1. Ship self-serve; instrument activation and usage
2. Define product-qualified leads: usage thresholds that predict purchase — seats added, feature depth, limits hit, multiple users per domain. One or two genuinely predictive behaviors beat a 20-signal score
3. Add sales when PQLs outstrip self-serve conversion: hand-raisers unanswered, teams stalling at free, enterprise features requested. First hires work the PQL queue only — sales assists the product's funnel, it doesn't replace it with outbound
4. Route by value: low-ACV PQLs get automated nudges; high-value accounts get a human

## Failure modes

- Hiring sales too early — outsourcing the learning; reps fail with no playbook and you can't tell whether the problem is the rep, the pitch, or the product
- PLG for a high-touch product — long time-to-value plus committee buying means free users never activate; the free tier becomes support cost, not funnel
- Motion copied from a different ACV band — Slack's PLG at $80K ACV, field sales at $3K. Copy companies with your economics, not your aspirations
- Outbound added to PLG before the PQL queue is mined — cold pipeline while warm in-product demand goes unworked
