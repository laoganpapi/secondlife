# Launch plan

## Tiers

Classify by expected business impact — never by engineering effort. A hard-to-build feature can still be a quiet release.

| Tier | What | Investment | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — major (1–3/year) | New product, new market, repositioning | Full campaign: press, analysts, customer stories, paid, event moment | 8–12 weeks |
| 2 — minor | Meaningful feature for the existing audience | Blog, segment email, demo video, sales refresh, in-product note | 4–6 weeks |
| 3 — silent | Improvements and fixes | Changelog, in-app note, docs — templatized | Days |

The tier gets decided at kickoff in one cross-functional call. The chronic failure is everything-as-Tier-1: team burnout, audience fatigue, diluted big moments.

## Campaign, not a day

- **Pre-launch (weeks–months):** build the audience you'll announce to — waitlist, beta community, teasers, analyst pre-briefs under embargo, references secured, pipeline warmed. A launch into no audience is a press release into the void.
- **Launch moment:** assets concentrated in a 24–72 hour window.
- **Post-launch nurture (4–12 weeks):** the phase that actually moves adoption — onboarding sequences, use-case content, retargeting, sales follow-up, second-wave customer stories. Budget as much effort for the 30 days after as for the day itself.

## Beta sequencing

Private alpha (design partners) → closed beta (waitlist — which doubles as pre-launch audience building; validates onboarding, collects quotable results) → open beta or GA.

Betas exist to generate the proof points the launch messaging needs. A Tier 1 launch with no customer evidence was sequenced wrong.

## Internal launch first

The company launches to itself 1–2 weeks ahead: sales and support trained and certified on the pitch, FAQ and objection handling distributed, pricing final, demo environment working, everyone knowing the date and embargo. A rep who can't explain the product on launch day converts external attention into confusion.

## Readiness checklist

Review one week out; any red item slips the date:

- **Product:** stable at expected load; onboarding tested end-to-end with fresh users; instrumentation live; rollback plan
- **Docs:** user docs, API reference, migration guides, current screenshots
- **Support:** trained team, macros, escalation path, launch-week staffing
- **Sales:** deck, one-pager, demo script, battlecards, pricing sheet, certified reps
- **Comms:** blog, press pitch, landing page, email sequences, social kit, video, customer quotes — all traced to the canonical messaging doc
- **Ops:** billing live, legal sign-off, tracking and dashboards ready

## Success metrics

- 2–3 metrics with targets and dates, chosen before launch and matched to intent — some launches are adoption or retention plays, not pipeline plays. Examples: "25% of target-segment users use the feature within 30 days"; signups by source; pipeline influenced; coverage for Tier 1.
- Review at week 1 (did it land), week 4 (is it working), week 12 (did it move the business), plus a retro that updates the playbook.

## Failure modes

Launch-day obsession with no follow-through — a signup spike that churns because nobody built the nurture phase · shipping the launch before onboarding works — launch amplifies whatever funnel exists, including a broken one · no pre-declared metric, so "it went well" by feel and nothing compounds · uniform Tier-1 treatment · skipped internal launch
