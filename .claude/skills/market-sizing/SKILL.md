# Market sizing

## Definitions, kept strict

| Term | Meaning | Test |
|---|---|---|
| TAM | Total revenue if every possible buyer bought | Would a buyer outside this set ever purchase? |
| SAM | The portion your product and geography can actually serve | Can you sell to them today, legally and operationally? |
| SOM | What you can realistically capture in a stated period | Backed by capacity, channel, and competition |

- Each number carries a unit, a period, and a date
- SOM is a forecast and must reconcile with the revenue model — a SOM the operating plan cannot produce is fiction

## Build both directions

**Bottom-up** — the defensible one:

```
number of buyers × purchase frequency × price = market revenue
```

- Buyers counted from an actual source: firmographic databases, licensing registries, census data, platform counts
- Price from observed pricing, not aspiration

**Top-down** — the sanity check:

- Start from a published industry figure, then narrow by segment, geography, and product fit
- Every narrowing step needs a stated ratio and its source

Reconcile the two. A gap larger than roughly 2× means an assumption is wrong — find it before presenting either.

## Penetration

- Never present "1% of a $50B market" — it asserts nothing and signals no analysis
- Ground the capture rate in something real: a comparable company's share at the same age, channel capacity, sales headcount × quota, or production limits
- State the time horizon; share without a date is meaningless

## Sanity checks

- Compare against the revenue of the largest incumbents — if TAM is smaller than a competitor's revenue, the definition is wrong
- Implied spend per buyer against known budgets — a figure exceeding a plausible budget line kills the estimate
- Growth rates against the underlying population growth
- Cross-check one segment against an independent source

## Sourcing

- Cite every input with source and date, inline
- Distinguish measured figures from estimates from assumptions, explicitly
- Vendor research reports carry incentive; note when a figure comes from a party selling into the market

## Output

Give a range with the two or three assumptions driving it, the bottom-up build shown step by step, and the reconciliation to top-down. A single confident number without a build is not a market size.
