# Positioning

## Definition

- Deliberate context-setting: what the product is best compared against, for whom, in which market frame. An input to messaging, pricing, and sales strategy — never the output.
- Weak positioning has symptoms: long sales cycles, losses to no-decision, prospects describing you wrong.

## Five components plus one

Build in this order — each feeds the next:

1. **Competitive alternatives** — what customers would actually do without you. Often "spreadsheet + intern" or "do nothing", not the named competitor. Start here, never with attributes.
2. **Unique attributes** — capabilities the alternatives lack. Provable, not aspirational.
3. **Value and proof** — the benefit each attribute enables, with evidence, clustered into 2–4 value themes rather than a feature list.
4. **Target segment** — who cares most intensely about that value, with identifiable characteristics. "Who cares a lot", not "who could use it".
5. **Market category** — the frame that makes the value obvious. Category triggers assumptions about competitors, pricing, and features — pick the frame whose assumptions work for you.
6. **Relevant trend** (optional) — urgency. Trend explains why now; category explains what you are. Never substitute one for the other.

## Process order

1. Study your best-fit, happiest customers — positioning derives from won deals, not wishes
2. Cross-functional team (sales, product, founders — marketing alone fails at handoff)
3. Let go of the frame inherited from the founding idea
4. List true alternatives from the customer's view
5. Isolate unique attributes → 6. map to value themes → 7. find who cares most
8. Choose the market frame that centers your strengths
9. Layer a trend, carefully
10. Capture in a canonical positioning document that downstream teams sign

## Category choice

| Style | Tradeoff |
|---|---|
| Head-on (win an existing category) | Zero education cost; only works if you can beat the leader on the market's existing criteria |
| Subcategory ("CRM for investment banks") | Inherits comprehension, redefines purchase criteria in your favor — the default for most startups |
| New category | Highest upside; you fund the education of an entire market — years and dollars most startups don't have |

## Testing

- Cheapest reliable test: the new positioning as a sales narrative in real calls — watch for faster "I get it", better-qualified pipeline, prospects repeating the frame back correctly.
- Secondary: does the segment self-identify from the description; can a stranger restate what you do after one read.
- Positioning that only works in a slide review has not been tested.

## Failure modes

- Positioning by internal opinion — founders voting on what they wish were true instead of deriving from won-deal analysis
- Category defaulting — keeping the category the product was born into, never re-examined as product and market shift. Positioning has a shelf life
- Trend-as-category — "AI for X" tells customers nothing about what you do, cost, or competitors. Trend on top of category, never instead
- Skipping to taglines — messaging debates that never converge are positioning debates in disguise
