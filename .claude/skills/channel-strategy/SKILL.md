# Channel strategy

## The 19 channels by route to the customer

- Paid and owned: search ads, social/display ads, offline ads
- Earned and content: SEO, content marketing, email, viral, PR, unconventional PR
- Outbound and relationships: sales, business development, affiliates
- Platform: existing platforms (app stores, marketplaces, piggybacking), engineering-as-marketing (free tools that draw the audience)
- Community and events: offline events, speaking, trade shows, community building
- Direct to the audience: the niche blogs and publications early adopters actually read

## The three rings

1. **Brainstorm all 19.** At least one plausible idea per channel before dismissing any. The point is countering channel bias — founders default to channels they know (engineers to SEO, MBAs to BD), and underused channels are underpriced precisely because competitors ignore them.
2. **Test ~3 cheaply, in parallel.** Each test answers three questions: what does a customer cost here (CAC), how many are plausibly available here (scale), and are they customers you want (conversion to retained use)?
3. **Focus on the one that works.** When a test converts, essentially all distribution effort goes into that single channel: at any stage, one channel dominates growth.
   - The others go dormant, not dead.
   - Nothing converts, so back to the outer ring. Repeated all-around failure is product-message signal, not just channel signal.

## Channel-model fit

A channel must fit the product *and* the unit economics:

| Channel class | Requires |
|---|---|
| Paid acquisition | LTV several multiples of CAC |
| Sales / BD | Four-to-five-figure ACV |
| Virality | A product used socially |
| SEO / content | Search volume plus a 6–12 month runway |

A channel can be a great channel and still be wrong for your economics.

## Test design

Per §13 discipline — exit condition before the loop starts:

- One hypothesis: "we can acquire signups from niche-blog sponsorships under $40 each"
- A spend cap (roughly $250–$1,000 at seed) and a time box (2–4 weeks)
- One primary metric declared in advance — CAC to activation, never clicks or impressions
- At cap or deadline the metric either clears the bar (→ focus) or doesn't (→ next channel)
- Tests are for information, not growth — never judged on revenue

## The 50% rule

Half of company effort goes to distribution from day one, parallel with product — not after launch. "Build it and they will come" is the most common startup death; channel tests should be running before the product ships.

## Saturation

- Every channel degrades: audiences saturate, ad costs rise as competitors arrive, algorithms shift, referral programs fatigue.
- Expect the bullseye channel to have a lifespan; re-run the process at each growth stage or when marginal CAC climbs. The channel that reached $1M rarely reaches $10M.

## Failure modes

Every channel at once — effort so thin nothing gets a real test · scaling on faith before a test converts (the spend cap exists for exactly this) · copying competitors' channels — their presence proves fit for their stage and economics, and contested channels are expensive · vanity-metric verdicts · abandoning a working channel for novelty before it saturates
