# Messaging hierarchy

## Rule zero

Messaging is positioning translated into words for an audience and a moment. If the message house keeps getting relitigated, the positioning underneath is unsettled — fix that; don't wordsmith harder.

## The message house

One page, canonical:

| Level | Content | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Roof — core promise | One sentence the target segment should remember, stated as customer outcome | Derived from the positioning's value themes, not product description |
| Pillars — 3, max 4 | One value theme each | Relevant to the segment, differentiated, provable. A pillar that could sit verbatim on a competitor's site isn't a pillar |
| Foundation — proof | Named-customer results with numbers, benchmarks, third-party validation, demo moments, certifications | Every claim carries proof or gets cut |

## The ladder

Feature → benefit → proof: "SOC 2 Type II (feature) means your security review takes days not months (benefit) — Acme's closed in 4 days (proof)."

- Feature-only messaging makes the reader do the translation.
- Benefit-only messaging is unfalsifiable fluff — "move faster", "work smarter".
- Every benefit statement survives the question "says who?"

## Variants without forking

One house, multiple doors:

- Vary per persona and stage: which pillar leads, vocabulary, proof selected, depth.
- Never vary: the core promise, the pillar set, the facts.
- A variant that needs a different roof is a different positioning — escalate, don't fork.
- All variants live in the same canonical document so drift is visible.

## Canonical doc

One owner, version-dated, containing: positioning summary, the house, persona variants, approved boilerplate at 25/50/100 words, banned and preferred vocabulary, the proof-point inventory with sources.

- Sales decks, website, ads, and PR all derive from it; anything contradicting it is a bug.
- Reviewed quarterly and at every positioning change.

## Testing, cheap before expensive

1. **5-second test** — show the hero to a cold prospect for five seconds; ask what the company does, for whom, why it's different. Failing question one means comprehension failed before persuasion started.
2. **Sales-call resonance** — the messaging in the first-call deck; track which pillars prospects lean into, repeat back, or fight. The highest-signal, lowest-cost lab; reps' words-that-work flow back into the doc.
3. **Ad copy A/B** — candidate value props as paid headlines with small budgets; ranks finalists by market pull. For choosing between finalists, not generating candidates.
4. **Language mining** — support tickets and win/loss quotes; customers' own phrasing usually beats invented copy.

## Failure modes

Messaging without positioning — endless copy cycles with no agreed alternatives, segment, or category · jargon accumulation until the page is legible only to employees · claims without proof ("enterprise-grade", "AI-powered") eroding the whole page · the everything-for-everyone roof no segment feels addressed by · fork drift — sales, web, and PR each evolving their own messaging until buyers hear three different companies
